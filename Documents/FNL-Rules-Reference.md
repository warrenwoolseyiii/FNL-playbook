## 0. Positions & Terminology

> ***Coach-supplied clarification — NOT stated in the league packet.*** *The FNL packet uses the terms "quarterback," "center," and "snap" without ever defining them. The definitions below were supplied by our head coach and are authoritative for our team's planning only. They are **not** league rule text.*

> ***Coach-supplied clarification — NOT stated in the league packet.*** ***The packet names no positions at all.*** *It specifies only that the game is **6 on 6**, that play begins with a Center-to-QB snap, and that the quarterback cannot run with the ball (plus "NO CENTER SNEAKS!!"). Every other position label below is **naming convention only** — borrowed from tackle football — and carries no rule weight. We are therefore free to rename any of these for our players (e.g., "snapper," "pitcher," "runner," "catcher") without changing anything legal about the play.*

### 0.1 Offensive Positions (coach-supplied naming convention)

- ***QB (Quarterback)*** — *The player who receives the hike/snap from the Center. Every play is started when the Center hikes the ball to the QB, so **the QB is the first player to get the ball on every play**. From there the QB may: hand the ball off to a runner (usually the RB), pitch the ball on a "Toss," or throw the ball — laterally or forward — as a pass.*
- ***QB running — pitched-back exception*** — *The QB **may not run with the ball on a normal snap-and-go**. However, once the ball has left him and is legally pitched, lateraled, or handed back to him, **he becomes an eligible runner and may advance the ball on the ground**. (Head coach ruling: "A QB can run if the ball is pitched back to him.") This parallels the packet's receiving rule in Section 7, which makes the QB an eligible receiver after a handoff/lateral/pitch behind the line of scrimmage — but the **running** case is **not stated in the league packet**.*
- ***Center*** — *The player who hikes (snaps) the ball to the QB to begin every play.*
- ***RB (Running Back)*** — *The player who usually receives the handoff from the QB.*
- ***C (Center) — expanded*** — *Purely a snapper. Because there is **no blocking**, the Center has no line responsibility after the snap; he is an eligible receiver like everyone else and can release into a route or a flat/checkdown.*
- ***WR (Wide Receiver)*** — *A player split wide toward a sideline, outside the Center/QB cluster, who releases into a route at the snap.*
- ***SR (Slot Receiver)*** — *A receiver aligned between a wide receiver and the Center — inside/off the line. Slot alignment gives easier inside releases and shorter, higher-percentage throws.*
- ***TE (Tight End)*** — *A receiver aligned immediately next to the Center. In flag this is purely an alignment label — with no blocking, a TE is just a close-in receiver.*
- ***RB (Running Back) — expanded*** — *Any player aligned behind the QB or beside him in the backfield. Receives handoffs, pitches, or releases as a receiver.*
- ***"Motion man" / "jet" — a role, not a position*** — *Any eligible player may be the one moving before the snap. Per the packet, **only one player is allowed in motion at a time** (Section 7), so "motion man" describes what a player is doing on a given play, not a personnel slot.*

#### Multiple Running Backs

> ***Coach-supplied clarification — NOT stated in the league packet.***

- *Using **two (or more) running backs at once is legal and common at the younger ages***. Nothing in the packet limits how many players may align in the backfield.
- *Multi-back sets exist to create **fake handoffs and misdirection** — the primary deception tools available to us. Typical uses: split-back / twin-back looks, counters, and one back faking while another carries.*
- *There is **no coverage cost** to keeping extra backs: per the packet, **all players are eligible to receive passes** (Section 7), so an extra RB is still a live receiving threat, not a wasted body.*
- *Because there is no blocking, extra backs are never "lead blockers" — they are decoys, ball carriers, or receivers only.*

### 0.2 Defensive Roles (coach-supplied naming convention)

- ***Rusher*** — *A defender who attacks the QB. Per the packet, any rusher **must start a minimum of seven yards from the line of scrimmage when the ball is snapped**, and **any number of players can rush** (Section 9).*
- ***Line-of-scrimmage defender / contain*** — *A defender who is not rushing and plays at the line. Per the packet, such players **may defend on the line of scrimmage only until the ball leaves the QB's hands**; once the ball is handed off, that restriction ends and all defenders may cross (Section 9).*
- ***Linebacker (LB)*** — *A defender playing in the middle level, between the line defenders and the deep defenders — responsible for underneath crossers and for runners breaking through.*
- ***Cornerback (CB)*** — *A defender assigned to a wide receiver or to an outside area.*
- ***Safety (S)*** — *The deepest defender, responsible for anything that gets behind the rest of the defense.*
- ***"Flag puller" — a job, not a position*** — *Every defender's primary duty is pulling flags. It is not a distinct role and should not be assigned as one.*

### 0.3 There Are No Linemen

> ***Coach-supplied clarification — NOT stated in the league packet.*** *There are **no linemen in the blocking sense on either side of the ball**, because the packet permits **NO BLOCKING** at all (Sections 3, 6, 9). The Center is **purely a snapper** — after the snap he is an eligible receiver, not a protector. No player on offense has a protection assignment, and no player on defense has a block to defeat.*

**Packet-sourced rules that reference these positions (unchanged):** the ball **must be snapped between the legs, not off to one side** (Section 6); **the quarterback cannot run with the ball** (Section 6, subject to the coach-supplied pitched-back exception above); **NO CENTER SNEAKS!!** (Section 6); **NO BLOCKING is allowed** (Sections 3, 6, 9); **only one player is allowed in motion at a time** and **all players are eligible to receive passes** (Section 7); rushers must start **seven yards** off the line and non-rushers may defend at the line **only until the ball leaves the QB's hands** (Section 9).

## 1. League Structure, Divisions & Eligibility

- FNL Flag Football is a **6 on 6** game.
- Divisions referenced in the packet: **K**, **1st/2nd Grade**, **3rd/4th Grade** (other divisions implied but not named).
- For divisions **K, 1st/2nd and 3rd/4th Grade — 1 Coach is allowed on field per team.**
- Only **registered players officially on a team roster as placed by the league Commissioner** are eligible to play.
  - Any unregistered/unplaced player "caught" participating results in **immediate forfeit** of that game and a **1 game suspension** for the coach.
  - **Subsequent violations result in the coach being removed from the league.**
- The sport is intended to be **"non-contact"**; however the packet states **"INCIDENTAL CONTACT DOES HAPPEN FREQUENTLY."**

## 2. Players, Rosters & Participation Requirements

- Teams must field a **minimum of 5 players at all times**.
- Most teams consist of a **maximum of 10 players** — **6 on the field**, with **3-4 substitutes**.

### Player Participation Requirement
Minimum participation time is followed in all FNL games **regardless of a player's attendance at practices**.

**9 Player Roster:**
- 1st Qtr: **3 players play offense only, 3 different players play defense only, and the other 3 players play the entire quarter**.
- 2nd – 4th Qtr: **3 different players sit each quarter**.

**8 Player Roster:**
- **2 different players sit each of the 4 quarters** — therefore each player plays **3 out of 4 quarters**.

### Penalty for Non-Compliance
If a coach fails to adhere and it is **caught prior to the end of the game**:
- **7 points awarded to the other team — each time the infraction is caught.**
- If it occurs in a **subsequent game**: **game forfeited**, win given to opposing team, and the **Head Coach receives a 1 game suspension**.

Referees will manage as best they can, but **it is ultimately the head coach's responsibility**. Any variation to this policy must receive **Commissioner approval prior to the start of the game** and be explained to the opposing head coach.

### Play Advantage Rule (8-10 vs. 7 only)
- If one team has **8-10 players** and the other has **7 players**, the coach with 7 players **must notify the Referee prior to the start of the game**.
- The referee notifies the other coach, who then has the **OPTION to identify which of the 7 players MUST sit one quarter**.
- The 7-player team's coach **decides which quarter** that player sits.
- If not identified prior to the start of the game and later recognized during the game, the 8-player team's coach **can still identify** the player that must sit.
- It is **both the Referee's and the 7-player Coach's responsibility** to recognize and identify this prior to the start of the game.

## 3. Game Format & Basics

- A **coin toss determines first possession**.
- The offense takes possession at its **5 yard line** and has **3 plays to cross midfield** for a 1st down.
- Once a team crosses midfield, it has **3 plays to score a touchdown**.
- If the offense fails to cross midfield, **possession changes** and the opposition starts its drive from **its 5 yard line**.
- **All possession changes, except interceptions, start on the offense's 5 yard line.**
- **Interceptions may be returned.**
- Teams **change sides after halftime**. Possession changes to the **loser of the coin toss**.
- **There are NO KICKOFFS**, and **NO BLOCKING is allowed**.
- **"No running zones"** exist at midfield and near each goal line (see Section 6).
- Motto: **"COACHES COACH – PLAYERS PLAY – REFEREES REF – FANS CHEER."**

## 4. Timing, Timeouts & Overtime

- Games are played with **10 minute running quarters (40 minutes total)**.
- **1 minute water break between quarters.**
- **5 minute halftime.**
- **30 seconds to snap the ball** each time the ball is spotted. Teams **may** receive one warning before a delay of game penalty is enforced.
- **2 timeouts per team, per half**; unused timeouts **do not carry over** to the 2nd half.
- **1 TIMEOUT PER TEAM IN EACH OT.**

### Overtime
If the score is tied at the end of 40 minutes, teams move directly into overtime — **EXCEPT 1ST/2ND GRADE AND 3RD/4TH GRADE DIVISIONS, WHICH WILL REMAIN A TIE**, with an **exception for Playoffs and TOC, where a winner must be determined**.

Tie-breaker system:
- **Coin flip determines possession.**
- Ball is placed at **Midfield**.
- After **3 plays**, the referee marks where the ball ended up (cone, flag, etc.).
- Opposing team takes over at **Midfield** and runs their **3 plays going in the same direction**.
- **Whichever team gains the most yards on 3 plays wins.**
- If both teams are tied after 3 plays, repeat until there is a winner.
- **Negative net yards on offense count against you.** The other team must still run their 3 plays.
- **If the ball is intercepted and returned for a touchdown, the game is over.**

## 6. Offensive Rules — Running & Ball Movement

- **The quarterback cannot run with the ball.**
  - *Coach-supplied reading (not packet text): since the QB is the player who receives the snap, this means **the snap-receiver may not advance the ball on the ground** on a normal snap-and-go. See Section 0 and item 12 in Section 16.*
  - ***Coach-supplied clarification — NOT stated in the league packet:*** *the restriction applies only while the QB still holds the snap. **Once the ball leaves him and is legally pitched, lateraled, or handed back to him, the QB becomes an eligible runner and may advance the ball on the ground.** (Head coach ruling: "A QB can run if the ball is pitched back to him.") Not league-confirmed.*
- **Handoffs, laterals and pitches are allowed as per NFL rules.**
- **NO CENTER SNEAKS!!**
  - *Coach-supplied reading (not packet text): the Center is the player who hikes the ball to the QB; he may not keep and advance the ball himself instead of snapping it back.*
- **No-Running zones** located **approx. 5 yards from each end zone (as marked)** and **approx. 5 yards on either side of midfield (as marked)**, designed to avoid short yardage, power-running situations.
- The player who takes the **handoff, lateral or pitch can throw the ball from behind the line of scrimmage**.
- Once the ball has been **handed off, lateraled or pitched, all defensive players are eligible to rush**.
- **Spinning is allowed and players CAN LEAVE THEIR FEET.**
- **The ball is spotted where the ball carrier's feet are when the flag is pulled, not where the ball is.**
- A player running with the ball must make an effort to avoid **INTENTIONALLY** running over or initiating aggressive contact on a defensive player. Penalty, if called, is **unsportsmanlike conduct** (see also **Charging**: 10 yards and loss of down).
- **NO BLOCKING is allowed** anywhere.
- The ball **must be snapped between the legs, not off to one side**, to start play.

## 7. Receiving Rules

- **All players are eligible to receive passes**, including the QB **if the ball has been handed off, lateraled or pitched behind the line of scrimmage** (per NFL Rules).
- As in the NFL, **only one player is allowed in motion at a time**.
- A player must have **at least one foot inbounds** when making a reception.

## 8. Passing Rules

- The QB has a **seven second "pass clock"**. If the QB does not get rid of the ball within seven seconds, **play is dead, loss of down**.
- Once the ball is handed off, the **seven second rule no longer is in effect**.
- **Shovel passes are allowed.**
- **Interceptions may be returned.**
- **Illegal forward pass** (pass thrown beyond the line of scrimmage): **5 yards and loss of down**.

## 9. Defensive Rules — Rushing the Quarterback

- All players who rush the QB must be a **minimum of seven yards from the line of scrimmage (as marked by ref) when the ball is snapped**.
- **Any number of players can rush the QB.**
- Players **not** rushing the QB **may defend on the line of scrimmage only until the ball leaves the QB's hands**.
- Once the ball is handed off, the **seven-yard rule no longer is in effect**, and **all defenders may go behind the line of scrimmage**.
- A **special marker, or the referee**, designates seven yards from the line of scrimmage.
- **NO BLOCKING, TACKLING, OR STRIPPING OF THE BALL IS ALLOWED.**
- **Illegal rushing** (starting rush from inside the 7-yard marker): **10 yards and automatic 1st down**.

## 10. Dead Balls & Spot of the Ball

Play is ruled **dead** when:
- Ball carrier's **flag is pulled**
- Ball carrier **steps out of bounds**
- **Touchdown or safety** is scored
- Ball carrier's **knee hits the ground**
- Ball carrier's **flag falls out**
- Ball carrier's **flags are not positioned on the side of the hips** (slid back toward the tailbone). This is **very subjective and up to the ref** to determine severity. **Warnings MAY be given instead of dead ball.** It is the **Coach's and Player's responsibility to check this throughout the game.**

Additional notes:
- **THERE ARE NO FUMBLES** (therefore **NO STRIPPING OF THE BALL**). **The ball is spotted where the ball hits the ground.**
- **BALL IS NOT DEAD IF THE BALL CARRIER'S HAND TOUCHES THE GROUND.**
- Ball is spotted **where the ball carrier's feet are when the flag is pulled**, not where the ball is.

## 17. Coaching Implications for 6-7 Year Olds

*Brief note — these are the factual rules above that most constrain play design. No new rules are introduced here.*

- **QB cannot run (on the snap) + no center sneaks:** every play must move the ball via pass, handoff, lateral, or pitch. Design around ball distribution, not QB keepers.
- **QB is an eligible runner once the ball is pitched back to him** (*coach-supplied clarification, Sections 0 and 6 — not packet text*): this makes QB-throwback, reverse-to-QB, and pitch-back concepts legally available to us. Caution: pitches are a high-risk mechanic for 6-7 year olds, so any such play needs heavy rehearsal and a short, flat pitch distance before it is used in a game.
- **Every play begins with a Center-to-QB exchange** (*per the coach-supplied definition in Section 0, not packet text*): the snap is the single highest-risk mechanic at this age. Combined with the packet rules that the ball must be snapped between the legs and that there are no fumbles, snap execution deserves the largest share of practice time.
- **No-run zones (approx. 5 yards from each end zone and approx. 5 yards on either side of midfield):** goal-line and first-down-line situations must be pass plays. Plan pass concepts specifically for these two field areas.
- **7-yard rush line:** the QB has real time and space before a rusher arrives, and non-rushers can only defend at the line until the ball leaves the QB's hands — quick-developing routes are open, but the pocket is generous.
- **7-second pass clock:** route timing must resolve quickly; no deep-developing concepts that need more time. Handoff first, and the clock is off.
- **No blocking at all:** no pulling linemen, no lead blockers, no screens with blockers. Ball-carrier plays rely on misdirection and space.
- **Multi-RB sets with fake handoffs are our primary misdirection tool** (*coach-supplied, Section 0 — not packet text*): with no blocking available, deception has to come from ball handling and backfield action. Caution: fakes require clean, repeatable mesh points, so they need heavy rehearsal at this age before they are worth calling in a game.
- **Once the ball is handed off/lateraled/pitched, all defenders may rush and cross the line of scrimmage** — a handoff instantly removes the QB's protection cushion.
- **Ball spotted at the ball carrier's feet when the flag is pulled** — reaching the ball forward gains nothing.
- **No fumbles; ball spotted where it hits the ground** — dropped exchanges are low-risk, which favors more handoffs/pitches at this age.
- **Only one player in motion at a time** — limits pre-snap shifts.
- **Flag guarding = 10 yards and loss of down** — teach hands-away running before anything else.
- **3 plays to cross midfield, 3 plays to score** — every series is short; plays must be high-percentage, not exploratory.
- **1 coach on the field** for K and 1st/2nd Grade — plays must be callable and correctable from on-field.
- **Participation policy (8 or 9 player rosters)** — plays must be runnable by every player, not just a few, since personnel rotates every quarter.
