FNL Playbook — Version 1
6-7 Year Old Friday Night Lights Flag Football  •  One Formation. Three Plays. Two Base Defenses — FORT (Man) & FENCE (2-3-1 Zone), starting with FORT.

Color = Where

BLUE = run at the blue cone
GOLD = run at the gold cone

Action = What

SWEEP — wide & fast
GHOST — fake, then give it back
BUBBLE — short catch, then run
Cones flip every time we change ends, so "BLUE" always means the same thing to a 6-year-old: run at the cone you can see.
Diagram key: every diagram in this book is drawn with downfield = up the page (toward the opponent's end zone). Backs, QB and Center start below the line of scrimmage; any real run path must end pointing toward the top of the diagram.

Formation: STAR

Same 6 spots, every single play. QB under center — hand-to-hand snap, never shotgun.
#SpotWhere you find it (kid words)
1CENTER (C)Stand on the ball.
2QBDirectly behind the Center, hands under him, toes almost touching his heels.
3BLUE BACK (BB)Directly behind the QB, one big step back, one small step toward the blue cone — tight split-back look.
4GOLD BACK (GB)Directly behind the QB, one big step back, one small step toward the gold cone — tight split-back look.
5BLUE WIDE (BW)On the blue sideline, even with the ball.
6GOLD WIDE (GW)On the gold sideline, even with the ball.

Why under center, not shotgun

The Center hikes the ball up into the QB's hands, hand-to-hand — the ball never flies through the air, so a dropped snap is nearly eliminated. Rushers must start 7 yards off the line, so the QB has time even with his back briefly to the defense. Ref §6, §9. Confirm hand-to-hand exchange legality with the league before the first game (Ref §16 item 21).

Why the split backfield (tight, behind the QB)

Both backs now start close together, directly behind the QB, instead of spread wide beside him. With the backs tight and symmetric, the QB's fake and his real handoff look identical to the defense — same depth, same angle, same body language. The mesh points sit almost on top of each other, so the QB barely has to move to reach either back, which is exactly what makes the fake believable at this age. Tradeoff: both backs now start farther from the sideline, so BLUE SWEEP / GOLD GHOST take a beat longer to reach the edge — see each play's "When to call it."

BLUE SWEEP

Type: RUN — our base play. Get wide first, then go north.
Ball carrier's real path Fake / decoy
CENTER: Hike the ball up into the QB's hands, then run five steps straight ahead and look for the ball.
QB: Feel the ball, open your feet toward the blue cone, and put the ball in the Blue Back's belly on your first step — the tight split-back spot means the mesh happens almost immediately.
BLUE BACK: Take the ball and run wide toward the blue cone, then turn upfield — your first step matters more now, since you start closer to the middle.
GOLD BACK: Run hard the other way toward the gold cone so they chase you — step away cleanly so you don't collide with the Blue Back leaving the tight spot.
BLUE WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision.
GOLD WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision.

When to call it

First play of a series, any time we need normal yards. Not in a no-run zone (5 yards off each end zone / 5 yards either side of midfield) — call BLUE BUBBLE there instead. Tradeoff: the tight split backfield starts the Blue Back farther from the sideline, so it takes a beat longer to reach the edge.

Coaching point

"Get wide FIRST, then go north." To the QB: "Feel it, first step, give." First step clean, away from your brother back — the two backs must not tangle up leaving the tight spot. Hands away from your flags — no flag guarding. Wides: count your own 5 steps, then break in — don't drift back toward the line of scrimmage.

GOLD GHOST

Type: RUN with a fake — BLUE SWEEP's twin. Only works after we've shown BLUE SWEEP.
Ball carrier's real path Fake / decoy
CENTER: Hike the ball up into the QB's hands, then run five steps ahead and look for the ball.
QB: Secure the snap, open to the blue cone and fake to the Blue Back on your first step, then spin back and hand it for real to the Gold Back on your second step — sell both meshes at the same depth.
BLUE BACK: Run the same path as BLUE SWEEP with empty hands and pretend you have the ball.
GOLD BACK: From the tight spot barely move on the fake, count one quick beat, then take the handoff and turn upfield toward the gold cone — the sequence is quicker and tighter now.
BLUE WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision.
GOLD WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision.

When to call it

After BLUE SWEEP has worked once or twice and the defense is flying to the blue side. Great on second down when we need a chunk. Not in a no-run zone. Tradeoff: the Gold Back also starts farther from the sideline now, so this play is best called once the defense is already flowing hard to blue — that flow buys back the extra beat.

Coaching point

"The fake runs just as hard as the ball." To the QB: "Ball, fake, give — same depth, same look." Both backs must not collide leaving the tight spot. Rehearse the mesh at half speed a lot before calling it in a game. Wides: count your own 5 steps, then break in — no drifting back toward the line of scrimmage.

BLUE BUBBLE

Type: PASS — short, easy. Receiver already faces the QB before the ball comes.
Ball carrier's real path Pre-snap motion Pass path
CENTER: Hike the ball up into the QB's hands, then run five steps ahead and turn around.
QB: Secure the snap, step straight back two steps, then toss the ball soft and low to the Blue Back right away.
BLUE BACK: From the tight split-back spot, walk toward the blue cone before the snap, then stop, turn around, and face the QB with hands up.
GOLD BACK: Stay in the tight spot directly behind the QB and face him — if the ball comes to you, catch it and run.
BLUE WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision, to pull the cornerback away from the edge.
GOLD WIDE: Count 5 steps straight upfield, then break in toward the middle, flat across the QB's vision, to pull the cornerback away from the edge.

When to call it

Inside a no-run zone (near either end zone or midfield) — those spots must be pass plays. Also any time we need a first down and the defense has crowded the run.

Coaching point

"Turn around EARLY, hands up, eyes on the ball." To the QB: "Ball, back-back, throw." Blue Back: walk a clean path away from the Gold Back leaving the tight spot — no crossing through him. Yards come after the catch, not in the air. Wides: count your own 5 steps, then break in flat — no drifting back toward the line of scrimmage.

FORT (Man Coverage — Starting Defense)

Six defenders, six jobs. One blitzer, two hawks, two man corners, one deep safety — cover your person, not a box.
Offense (circle) Defense (square) Man coverage link
BLITZ: Start on the 7-yard mark and sprint straight at the QB on every snap.
BLUE HAWK: Stand your ground on the blue side of the line — don't cross until the ball leaves the QB's hands.
GOLD HAWK: Stand your ground on the gold side of the line — don't cross until the ball leaves the QB's hands.
BLUE CB: Stick to your one receiver like glue — go where he goes.
GOLD CB: Stick to your one receiver like glue — go where he goes.
SAFETY: Own the deep middle grass — nobody gets behind you, ever.

Adjustments

3+ receivers: SAFETY converts to man on the 3rd WR — deep field opens up (accepted tradeoff at this age). Fewer than 2 WRs: spare CB shifts inside to help contain the run; SAFETY always stays home deep.

Coaching points

BLITZ starting inside the 7-yard mark = illegal rushing (10 yds, auto 1st down). HAWKS crossing early = offsides (5 yds, auto 1st down). CORNERS: eyes on your guy, not the fake. SAFETY: pass first, come down last.

Why FORT — our starting defense

Constant QB pressure from a fixed, legal 7-yard depth exploits the 7-second pass clock (Ref §8) without risking an illegal rush. One clear job per kid — a person to cover, not a box. Honest tradeoff: tight man corners defend edge sweeps well, but the deep field is exposed the moment the offense shows 3+ receivers. Ref §9, §11 (7-yard rush minimum, offsides/illegal rushing penalties), §8 (pass clock).

FENCE (2-3-1 Zone — Alternate Defense)

Six defenders, six jobs. 2 line, 3 linebackers (MIKE blitzes), 1 deep safety — cover your box, not a person.
Offense (circle) Defense (square) Zone third
MIKE (blitzer): Start on the 7-yard mark and sprint straight at the QB on every snap.
BLUE LINE: Stand your ground on the blue side of the line — don't cross until the ball leaves the QB's hands.
GOLD LINE: Stand your ground on the gold side of the line — don't cross until the ball leaves the QB's hands.
BLUE THIRD: This sideline-to-cone box is your grass — pull the flag of whoever comes into it.
GOLD THIRD: This sideline-to-cone box is your grass — pull the flag of whoever comes into it.
SAFETY: Own the deep middle grass — nobody gets behind you, ever.

Adjustments

3+ receivers: thirds still just cover their box — pull whoever enters, no extra defender needed. Heavy run look: LINE holds the edge; thirds may crash down only after the handoff. Near our goal line: SAFETY tightens up; thirds squeeze their boxes toward the sideline.

Coaching points

MIKE starting inside the 7-yard mark = illegal rushing (10 yds, auto 1st down). LINE crossing early = offsides (5 yds, auto 1st down). THIRDS: grass first, ball second — don't chase. SAFETY: pass first, come down last.

Why FENCE

Zone gives each kid one patch of grass instead of a moving person to track — far more coachable at 6-7. A blitzing MIKE from a fixed, legal 7-yard depth keeps constant pressure inside the 7-second pass clock (Ref §8) without ever risking an illegal rush. Honest weak spot: the gap between our LINE defenders and the THIRDS can be exploited by a fast edge sweep — same family of play as our own BLUE SWEEP / GOLD GHOST. Ref §9, §11 (7-yard rush minimum, offsides/illegal rushing penalties), §8 (pass clock).
